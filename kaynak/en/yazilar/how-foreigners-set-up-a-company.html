One of the most frequent questions from foreigners living in Türkiye, or planning to trade with it, is how to set up a company. The short answer: the process is almost the same as for Turkish citizens. The differences lie in the documents of foreign shareholders, bringing capital in from abroad, and the notifications after formation. This guide takes the steps in order.

1. Which type of company?

Foreign investors may set up any type of company provided for in the Turkish Commercial Code. In practice, two types stand out:

Limited company (Ltd. Şti.)Joint-stock company (A.Ş.)
Minimum capitalTRY 50,000TRY 250,000 (TRY 500,000 initial capital under the registered capital system)
Payment of capitalNo payment required before registration; the committed capital is paid laterAt least 25% of the cash capital is deposited in a bank before registration; the rest is paid within 24 months of registration (Commercial Code Art. 344)
ManagementManager(s)Board of directors
Share transfersNotarised agreement, general assembly approval and registration requiredEasier; as a rule, no registration required
Shareholders' liability for public debtsShareholders are liable, in proportion to their shares, for tax debts that cannot be collected from the companyNo such liability for shareholders; liability lies with the representatives

A limited company is usually sufficient for a small or medium-sized trading activity. If more shareholders, frequent share transfers or outside investors are expected, a joint-stock company is more suitable.

2. Documents required from foreign shareholders

Foreign individual shareholder:

  • A copy of the passport translated into Turkish by a sworn translator and certified by a notary;
  • A potential tax identification number (obtained from a tax office or the Revenue Administration website);
  • For those with a Turkish residence permit, the foreigner identification number and address.

Foreign corporate shareholder (a company abroad):

  • A current certificate of good standing or registry extract from the company's home country;
  • A resolution of the board or competent body approving the formation of, and participation in, a company in Türkiye;
  • A power of attorney for the person who will represent the company in Türkiye, and that person's signature declaration;
  • Originals of all these documents with an apostille (or consular legalisation for countries that are not party to the Apostille Convention), together with notarised Turkish translations.

Documents from abroad usually take the longest in the formation timeline. The document list should therefore be prepared first and apostille procedures started early.

3. Formation steps

  1. Trade name and field of activity: The company name and its activities (NACE codes) are determined.
  2. MERSİS application: The formation data are entered into the Ministry of Trade's MERSİS system. A foreign shareholder is registered with a passport number or foreigner identification number. The articles of association are generated automatically from this data.
  3. Capital: In a joint-stock company, at least one quarter of the cash capital is deposited in a special bank account opened for the formation. The bank releases it to the company after registration. If capital comes from abroad, the transfer should be described as "capital" and a foreign-exchange purchase certificate obtained from the bank; this document proves that the capital was brought into Türkiye.
  4. Signatures: The articles of association and signature declarations are signed at the trade registry office or before a notary. A sworn translator attends for shareholders who do not speak Turkish.
  5. Registration: The company is registered by the trade registry office and announced in the Turkish Trade Registry Gazette. The company acquires legal personality upon registration. The Competition Authority fee (0.04% of capital) is paid at this stage.
  6. Tax office: Registration details are sent electronically to the tax office, which inspects the business premises. An electronic notification (e-tebligat) account and a registered e-mail (KEP) address are also opened for the company.

4. After formation: obligations specific to foreign capital

  • E-TUYS notifications: Foreign-owned companies register in the Ministry of Industry and Technology's E-TUYS system. Transactions such as formation, capital increases and share transfers are notified through this system. In addition, an annual activity information form for foreign direct investments is submitted by the end of May each year.
  • Beneficial owner declaration: The individuals who ultimately own the company are declared to the tax administration. Where the shareholder is a company abroad, the chain is followed up to the individuals behind it.
  • Bank account: Banks review the documents of shareholders and beneficial owners of foreign-owned companies in more detail. Opening an account may take several weeks; complete documents shorten the process.
  • Accounting and returns: From formation, the company keeps books and files returns under Turkish tax law. If regular reports to foreign shareholders are needed, their format and language should be agreed at the outset.

5. Work permit and residence

Setting up or owning a company does not by itself give the right to live or work in Türkiye.

  • When is a work permit needed? If a foreign shareholder will actually work in the company as a manager, executive or employee, a work permit is required. It is not required for non-resident board members of a joint-stock company or for shareholders of a limited company who are not managers.
  • Criteria for shareholders: Under the Ministry of Labour and Social Security's criteria, for a foreign shareholder the company's paid-in capital must be at least TRY 500,000, and the foreigner's share must be at least TRY 500,000 and at least 20%. At least five Turkish citizens must be employed, from the seventh month of the first permit. These capital and employment criteria do not apply to a foreign shareholder whose share is USD 100,000 or more.
  • Residence: A valid work permit serves as a residence permit. Shareholders who will not work apply for residence separately.

Short checklist

  • Have the company type and capital been decided?
  • Are the passport translations and potential tax numbers of foreign shareholders ready?
  • If there is a foreign corporate shareholder, are the registry extract, resolution and power of attorney apostilled and translated?
  • If capital comes from abroad, have the transfer description and foreign-exchange purchase certificate been planned?
  • Are documents ready for E-TUYS registration, the beneficial owner declaration and the bank account?
  • Does a foreign shareholder who will work in the company meet the work permit criteria?
